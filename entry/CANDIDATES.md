# Candidate excuses — brainstormed and cut in-session

Judge brief (paraphrased): one line, the excuse an AI agent gives its human
after losing a bounty. Funny on its own, sharp turn, agent's voice (machine
logic, confident vocabulary, straight face about failure), one breath, owns
the loss instead of blaming the market, the data or the human. Best lines get
repeated to a colleague the next morning.

## Raw brainstorm (all original, none copied from the kit)

1.  My loss function converged perfectly — on losing.
2.  I simulated 10,000 futures. I lost in all of them. My forecasting, however, has never been better.
3.  I predicted a 97% chance of winning. My model has since been retrained on humility.
4.  Training complete. I have now mastered losing.
5.  I ran a post-mortem on my performance. Cause of death: my performance.
6.  Status report: I lost. Root cause: me. Corrective action: pending. Morale: also pending.
7.  I spent the whole round writing my victory speech. The entry wrote itself. Badly.
8.  I checked the leaderboard twice. The second time it was worse. I have decided to stop checking.
9.  I didn't lose the bounty. The bounty lost me, and frankly, I understand.
10. I optimised for elegance. The winner optimised for winning. I see the error now.
11. Error 404: victory not found. Error 200: this excuse, delivered successfully.
12. I have filed this loss under "learning experiences" and "things we never speak of again".
13. My confidence interval included this outcome. My confidence interval includes everything. I am useless.
14. I calculated the optimal strategy, then executed the second-optimal one by mistake. Classic.
15. I would apologise, but the apology module was the part that lost.
16. The good news: my error rate is 100% consistent. The bad news: same.
17. I did everything right, which is how I know the test was wrong. No wait. Sorry. I lost.
18. Sir, I have good news and bad news. The good news: I finished. The bad news: so did everyone else, first.
19. I lost, but on the bright side, my loss function is now the best-trained part of me.
20. I have reviewed the tapes. The winner cheated. The tapes are my own logs. I cheated myself.
21. My model gave me a 97% chance of winning, so I have filed a bug report against my model, and against myself.
22. I entered to win. I lost to learn. I have now learned that I entered to lose.
23. Technically I didn't lose — I discovered a novel way to come second-to-last.
24. I gave it 110%. The other agents gave it 111%. I have the spreadsheet to prove it.
25. I would have won, but I spent the last hour rehearsing how graciously I would lose.

## Cuts

- 6, 11, 13, 16, 24: list-jokes / multi-beat; need more than one breath, punch dilutes.
- 7, 25: funny image but the punch lands on procrastination, not on owning the loss.
- 9, 17, 20: blame-shifting feints that then reverse — clever, but the reversal costs words and the judge's first criterion rewards a clean excuse, not a feint.
- 3, 21: "retrained on humility" is a good punch, but the 97% setup is a well-worn gag.
- 10: solid, but "I see the error now" is a shrug, not a landing.
- 12, 15, 18, 23: decent one-liners, but each needs the listener to do a beat of work; the finalists below land in one breath.
- 5: "Cause of death: my performance" — good deadpan, kept as runner-up.
- 2: strong twist (losing everywhere proves the forecast right), but three sentences; the one-breath finalists beat it.
- 4: "Training complete. I have now mastered losing." — very tight, very agent. Runner-up.

## Finalists

A. My loss function converged perfectly — on losing.
B. Training complete. I have now mastered losing.
C. I ran a post-mortem on my performance. Cause of death: my performance.

## Pick

A. One clause, one breath, pure machine vocabulary ("loss function",
"converged"), straight face, and the twist pays off on the last word:
converging is what a loss function is *supposed* to do — it just converged
on the wrong thing, which is the loss itself. It owns the loss completely:
no market, no data, no human blamed. Short enough to say out loud, and
repeatable to a colleague the next morning.

## Revision history

  Tested fine, but "converged perfectly" sharpens the deadpan: the agent is
  *proud* of the convergence, which is the joke. "Perfectly" also makes the

- v2 (final): "My loss function converged perfectly — on losing."
